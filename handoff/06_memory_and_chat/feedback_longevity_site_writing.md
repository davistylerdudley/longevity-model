Davis pushed back several times on the same things across the longevity project:
- **Cut wordiness; raise comprehensibility.** Simple explanation/scenario first, technical detail after. No AI tells: avoid em dashes and words like "genuinely", "fundamentally", "qualitatively different".
- **Real citations only** — verify DOIs/URLs before writing them (WebSearch first). Credibility is the point.
- **Narrative sections must be narrative.** The AI-2027-inspired scenario must read as a story with recurring people, dated chapters, reversals, and concrete specifics — not a listing of facts and considerations. When he says "make it more concrete/illustrative", he means named tech, scenes, objects, dates, specific places (NYC, Uttar Pradesh).
- **Tone:** optimistic but grounded; never open with "most people imagine immortality, they're wrong".
- **Completeness standard:** ship the finished thing with tests, docs, and a QA pass; never offer to table work or present a workaround when the real fix is reachable.

**Why:** he's building a public, credibility-sensitive artifact and reviews it as a careful reader would.
**How to apply:** default to tight prose, verify every source, write scenario content as fiction with continuity, and finish with build + tests + headless screenshots before saying it's done. See [[project-longevity-model]].
